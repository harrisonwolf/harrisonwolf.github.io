Partial-sum spiral — what you are looking at

A complex series Σ a_n(s) is drawn as the polygon of its partial sums S₀ = 0, S₁ = a₁, S₂ = a₁ + a₂, … in the output plane (right). For ζ(s) = Σ n^(−s) each segment has length n^(−σ) and turns by −t·ln n, so the shape is governed by σ = Re s (shrinkage) and t = Im s (twist). The domain plane (left) is where you choose s; its colouring is the reference f(s) itself (hue = argument), not the partial sum. The orange ring is f(s), computed independently (Borwein / Euler–Maclaurin), so you can watch the sum approach it — or fail to.

Landmarks the readout prints

n₁ = |t|/2π is the last stationary point of the phase: below it the path is a chain of curlicues (Cornu-type eyes) whose centres are the vertices of the dual sum of the approximate functional equation; above it the path winds around f(s) on a near-circle of radius R_N = N^(−σ)/(2 sin(t/2N)) with turn t/N per step (a regular q-gon when N = q·n₁, a straight-through at N = |t|/π). The EM tail appends the Euler–Maclaurin correction terms as extra segments, which make even a divergent Dirichlet series land on its analytic continuation.

Panes and controls

terms N — log slider to 3·10⁸; animate doubles N every 1.2 s. View — what the camera follows (structure / orbit / tip / whole path / locked); pan and zoom lock it. markers — osculating circle and its centre, predicted eye centre (◇). Rolling plane — the image under f of a line, circle, custom s(t) or hand-drawn curve, lightness-coded by the curve parameter; for a closed curve the readout gives the winding number (zeros − poles enclosed). 3-D lift — vertex n raised to z = n·Δz or Δz·ln n (the final spiral becomes a helix). compute — Web Workers in the page or the Python backend (spiral_server.py).

Precision

Term phases are computed in double-double arithmetic and checkpoints are compensated, so every vertex is exact to a few ulps of the term size for |t| up to ~10¹⁵; the readout says when terms fall below the ulp of |S_N|. The self-test at the bottom recomputes the embedded test vectors against the Python engine.
