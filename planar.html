Computational geometry · C++ · standard library only

Meshes from first principles.

Triangulation is easy on clean input and fragile on real input: collinear points, duplicates and near-touching edges are where meshes crack. This engine triangulates polygons and builds Delaunay meshes and Voronoi regions with no geometry library to lean on, deciding those edge cases explicitly and pinning them down with 1,027 test assertions.

Why it exists

The nominal algorithms are easy. The boundary cases are not.

Meshes and Voronoi regions underpin games, maps and simulations, and they fail in subtle ways when rounding misjudges which side of a line a point lies on. The predicates here handle collinear inputs, duplicate points and orientation with epsilon checks scaled to the size of the problem, so a near-touch resolves without a crossing or a degenerate mesh.

  • Scale-aware predicates.Every orientation and in-circle test weighs its determinant against an epsilon sized to the input, not a constant.
  • Verdicts with evidence.With tracing on, each decision prints the determinant, the epsilon and the call, so a wrong mesh can be traced to the test that allowed it.
  • Euler-exact.Triangle and edge counts are checked against Euler’s formula on every run; the validation pass re-proves every circumcircle empty.
  • Who wrote what.I hand-wrote the primitives, polygon operations and original ear clipper; I directed and reviewed coding agents on the current Delaunay/Voronoi layers, harness, viewers and benchmarks.
Triangulation

One polygon, and the near-touch inside it.

A random 15-vertex polygon from the engine’s generator, cut into 13 triangles by the ear clipper: n − 2, as a simple polygon must give. Two of its edges pass about 0.06 units apart without crossing, and the triangulation leaves no gap and no overlap there.

Predicates, caught deciding

Every verdict prints its evidence.

Collinear input is rejected with the decision logged: orient2d equals zero against the scaled epsilon, three times, then a refusal instead of a degenerate mesh. On near-degenerate points the in-circle tests print centre, radius², margin and epsilon before calling INSIDE or outside; margins swing from millions down to about 50, which is where the epsilon scaling earns its keep.

Delaunay & Voronoi

Sites to mesh to dual, at a legible scale.

Seventy sites, seed 7. Every Voronoi vertex is the circumcentre of a Delaunay triangle, often outside its own triangle, since only acute triangles contain their circumcentres.

Measured performance

Validation costs more than construction.

At 20,000 sites the Bowyer–Watson triangulation takes 5.6 s and the Voronoi dualization half a millisecond; then re-proving every circumcircle empty takes 10.1 s. The O(N²) check costs twice what it checks, and the engine runs it anyway. The benchmark studio keeps exact generated inputs, raw process output, per-run resource logs, manifests and checksums.
